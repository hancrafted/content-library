# Why AI gives you more work, and why you'll want it

## Format

Foundations. Three Slides, about three minutes at its core; progressive disclosure extends the same Slides to about ten minutes. A one- to two-minute YouTube Short is cut from it afterwards.

## Audience

The general public starting out with AI as consumers, the same audience as "Treat AI like a brilliant freelancer with amnesia"; the YouTube audience leans more technical.

## Style

Exhibition: the general public starting out with AI know the history of farming, the steam engine and the computer, so a wall of exhibits grounds the misconception in what they know, and the next misconception Episode looks like the same series.

## Learning goal

Explain why AI adds work rather than removing it, and name the management skills that enable you to do more complex tasks with it.

## Actionables

1. Next time you hand work to a colleague, give them a proper handoff with enough context.
2. When you onboard a new team member, treat it like opening a new AI session.
3. When you get a complex project, break it into smaller chunks and define the seams between them, so each task contributes to the overall goal.

## Hook

Today I want to clear up a misconception about AI: people asking whether AI will reduce their work, or speed it up. AI actually adds work, because with AI you can now perform much more complex tasks than you could on your own before, and managing those tasks is the work AI adds. The payoff is that you accomplish so much more, if you are willing to upskill and learn how to manage AI like a team.

## Red thread

AI reduces execution and forces you to engage with traditional management skills, which allows you to perform much more complex tasks.

## Analogy

You were promoted: you move from execution into management. The work doesn't shrink, it changes, and you can do more complex tasks than before.

### From execution to management

1. **Every revolution, one card** — with every innovation, a machine took over predictable work and people moved up to the unpredictable parts: judging, deciding, coordinating. Those who took up managing the machines became the managers, while those who stayed on the lines stayed workers, nothing wrong with that; with a seismic shift like AI, there is this potential for people to become the new managers. AI is the first tool that does part of that unpredictable work itself (natural language, reasoning, creativity, brainstorming), so you move up again, now managing something that thinks; one person can do tasks that used to need a whole team. _Visual:_ a row of cards (farming, the steam engine, the computer), then a much larger AI card. Each card has two layers: on top what the machine took over, below where people moved up (the plough digs, the farmer plans the harvest; the engine does the lifting, the worker plans the line; AI drafts and reasons, you manage it like a team). Progressive disclosure reveals the lower layer card by card, so the climb builds, and the AI card lands last as the biggest step. Under the steam engine card, a small group of workers who upskilled to manage the plant is catapulted into a different life, while the others stay factory workers, with no judgment; the AI card repeats the fork, open. _Research:_ 1, 2, 5, 7, 8
2. **You were promoted** — AI promotes you from execution into management, and that misalignment is the biggest one people face today: most still act as if they do the work rather than manage it. The skills to cross it are management skills, which Matt Pocock calls the language barrier. _Visual:_ a person moving from an "execution" box to a "management" box, with the gap between them labelled "the misalignment". Progressive disclosure fills the gap with four stepping stones (break it down, write clear goals, review, delegate); the last reveal names the gap the language barrier. _Research:_ 3, 4, 6
3. **Practise it today, at work** — these skills can be practised now with colleagues, so people skills become AI skills; more complex projects are now within reach, an invitation to grow, not a burden. _Visual:_ three rows mirroring a moment at work with its AI equivalent: hand off work to a colleague, or before a vacation, mirrors starting a new chat with context; onboard a new team member mirrors opening a new AI session; break down a big project mirrors splitting work into AI tasks. At rest only the work side shows; each click reveals the AI side. The closing line "more complex projects are now within reach" sits under the rows. _Research:_ 3

## Research questions

1. Why do productivity tools like the plough, the steam engine and the computer increase total work instead of reducing it?
2. Why does AI let individuals take on more complex tasks than before?
3. Why do delegation and management skills improve results when working with AI?
4. Why does Matt Pocock describe the skills for working with AI as a language barrier?
5. Why do language models, unlike earlier machines and tools, produce non-deterministic results?
6. Why do people working with AI keep executing the work themselves instead of managing it?
7. Why did workers who took up managing new machines, like the steam engine, become managers while others stayed workers?
8. Why did new technologies like the steam engine change social mobility for the workers who adopted them?
