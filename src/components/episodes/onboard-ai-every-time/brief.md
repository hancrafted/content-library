# Treat AI like a brilliant freelancer with amnesia

## Format

Foundations. No time cap for now; aims at about 10 minutes. Episode 1 of 3 in the intro to agentic workflows (then attention span, then promotion). Built as the reference later videos point back to.

## Audience

The general public: anyone starting to work with AI as a consumer, not engineers or data scientists. First deliveries: an accelerator cohort (students, pre-seed founders) on Friday, then non-technical office workers in German SMEs. They want to get reliable results from AI without learning how it works inside.

## Style

Studio: a general audience new to AI reads sleek, calm Slides as current, and the `ai` accent marks only what is AI.

## Learning goal

The core Episode lands one goal: name the five traits that make AI costly (PRICE), and manage them with five moves (BRIEF).

## Actionables

1. Break it down.
2. Requirements first.
3. Interview yourself with AI.
4. Evaluate each step.
5. Fresh start for each piece.

## Hook

Imagine you start a new job: new environment, new culture, new tools. Your first task is to organise next year's summer party for 200 employees across Europe. Picture every question you would need answered to do it well.

## Red thread

Working with AI is like onboarding a brilliant freelancer with amnesia, every time. The freelancer has five traits to manage, or you pay the PRICE; five moves, a BRIEF, manage them, and the venue walkthrough shows BRIEF in action. What is good for a human is also good for AI.

## Analogy

A brilliant freelancer with amnesia, every time, described by five traits that spell PRICE: Probabilistic, Relentless, Informed, Confident, Every word billed. Managed by five moves that spell BRIEF: Break it down, Requirements first, Interview yourself with AI, Evaluate each step, Fresh start for each piece.

## Constraints

- Every module stands alone: the grounding Slide, PRICE, BRIEF and the walkthrough. No module refers to another, so any can be dropped or reused; the Episode can start at PRICE when time is short.
- The one exception: any module may refer to the cornerstones (PRICE and BRIEF), since every Episode points back to them.
- Module order: grounding, PRICE, BRIEF, walkthrough.
- Talk first (design guide): every Slide shows its claim at rest. On the cornerstones the end state shows every column and mapping; step-through and hover only stage it in the talk.
- Stay high level: no mechanics of how to slice work (that is the promotion Episode).
- Non-technical throughout: say "word", not "token"; tokens may appear in a popover.
- Leave out ChatGPT's context and memory features; they belong to the attention span Episode. A short preamble in the voice-over says so.
- Reviewing techniques are taught in the promotion Episode. Here, evaluating a step means checking it against the requirements, and chunks you can review only shape how the work is split.
- Managing several freelancers gets one sentence at most, as a teaser for the promotion Episode.

## Research questions

1. Why does tacit organisational knowledge fail to transfer during employee onboarding?
2. Why do LLMs give different answers to the same prompt?
3. Why does an LLM resend the whole chat on every message?
4. Why does an LLM forget everything between chats?
5. Why do LLMs answer confidently instead of asking when a prompt is vague?
6. Why does an LLM trained on public text lack knowledge of a specific organisation?
7. Why does decomposing a complex task into smaller subtasks improve LLM results?
8. Why do explicit written requirements reduce the variation in LLM output?
9. Why does letting an LLM ask clarifying questions before a task improve the result?
10. Why does checking intermediate steps catch errors that a review of only the final result misses?
11. Why does a fresh, focused context outperform a long, accumulated chat?

## Open ideas

- The walkthrough's popover and modal wording is left to the Dossier.
- For the promotion Episode: slice work vertically, like tracer bullets, not by layer. On the venue: one agent builds the lead list, one agent per venue researches every criterion into a fixed output format, one agent compares. Slicing by layer (location, then capacity, then quotes) is the failure mode to show.
- Alternative acronym considered: STEER (Slice it, Tell your context, Expect questions, Essentials only, Record a spec).
- Two misconceptions become their own Short Episodes, each standalone and allowed to lean on PRICE; in a live workshop they can slot in between PRICE and BRIEF. Their settled beats:
  - Why AI is an accelerator, not a solution: misconception "AI solves your problems", clarification "AI amplifies what you give it, the good and the bad"; the over-eager colleague who took the wrong exit; 24/7 and ten times faster, execution is not value; steer, don't blame, encourage questions early; one sentence on a whole team of such freelancers.
  - Why working with AI takes more effort, just like with people: misconception "AI will reduce your work", clarification "AI lets you take on more complex work"; the plough, steam engine and computer each raised what one person could achieve; AI is the first tool for meaning, culture and language, so people skills become AI skills; a project that used to need an agency is now within reach; an invitation to grow, not a burden.
- Cornerstone: popovers carry the lesson; hover needs a click or step-through for video and mobile, one clean recordable state per trait.
- Cornerstone: many-to-many trait mapping (cap 2–3 moments per trait) and outer-column moments swappable per Episode. Observed but not drawn: Informed↔Confident, Relentless↔Every word billed.
- The Every word billed popover opens with "Every word billed:", where it once said "inflated cost".
- PRICE pun for the voice-over: "Forget the price, and you'll pay it every time."
- Whether the Token Economy block stays in the workshop.
- Second use case for the solution: the invitation to 200 employees, or the budget proposal for the boss.
- In a live workshop the thought-experiment questions can be asked interactively; the recording keeps them rhetorical.
