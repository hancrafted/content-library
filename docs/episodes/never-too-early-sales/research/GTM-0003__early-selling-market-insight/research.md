# Why does selling early yield market insight?

## Context

Asked to support the claim that a founder who starts selling before revenue is needed gains knowledge of the market, not only a head start on cash. The claim is about what direct contact with prospective buyers teaches, so the question is scoped to learning, not to revenue timing.

## Answer

A founder's starting picture of the market is a set of guesses, and an informed opinion is still a guess. Selling forces direct conversations with real prospects, which replace those guesses with facts about who the buyers are, how they work and what they need. Graham reports that the feedback from the earliest direct contact is the best a company will get, and Y Combinator treats talking to customers as part of the CEO's job. Early selling therefore buys insight that no amount of desk work inside the company provides.

## Findings

1. A company's own view of its customers is unreliable: "your initial model of users is always inaccurate, even if you're one of them" [^graham-do-things-that-dont-scale].
2. Direct engagement with the earliest users produces feedback that a founder says will be the best the company ever gets, and doing the work by hand teaches founders what to build [^graham-do-things-that-dont-scale].
3. Without customer contact, opinion fills the gap. Blank, then a marketer, was told "You've never talked to anyone in this market, you don't know who they are, you don't know what they need", and concluded he "didn't have any facts" [^blank-get-out-of-my-building].
4. The remedy was to go and talk to customers, to "find out who they are, how they work", with the instruction not to return until the team could be told something it did not already know [^blank-get-out-of-my-building].
5. Blank's lessons: "An intelligent opinion is still a guess", and "There are no facts inside the building so get the heck outside" [^blank-get-out-of-my-building].
6. Y Combinator's guidance is that the best founders keep a direct connection to users, and that for a CEO talking to customers is part of the job [^migicovsky-how-to-talk-to-users].
7. What yields insight is specifics, not pitches: the aim is to learn about problems the user has faced, by asking about things that already happened ("tell me about the last time you encountered this problem") rather than hypotheticals [^migicovsky-how-to-talk-to-users].

## Caveats

The sources are founder and investor practitioner accounts, not controlled studies, so they show what experienced practitioners report, not a measured effect size. Two of them (Graham, Migicovsky) concern talking to users and Blank concerns talking to customers; none isolates the act of selling from interviewing. That a sales conversation produces the same facts, because it is a direct conversation with a buyer, is an inference. The Migicovsky source is a Y Combinator recap of a lecture, not the original video. Blank's post does not report what he actually learned on the customer visits.
