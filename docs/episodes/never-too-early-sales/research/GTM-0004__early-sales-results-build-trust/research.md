# Why does engaging in sales and obtaining early sales results, like LOI, filled sales pipeline, generate trust?

## Context

Asked to support the claim that selling early earns a young company trust from investors and from prospective customers. The trust meant is the willingness of an outsider to commit money or purchasing risk to a firm with no track record. The question is scoped to why an early commercial result persuades, not to how much it is worth.

## Answer

Outsiders cannot observe the quality of a young company, so they read observable signals instead. An early sale, a letter of intent or a pipeline of real buyers is such a signal: another party spent scarce attention or money on it, which a weaker firm would find harder to obtain. It also shows ability, one of the three components of trustworthiness. Investors weigh the team above the product, so the strongest effect runs through the team's demonstrated ability to win buyers. No study tests letters of intent or pipelines directly, so applying the general findings to them is an inference.

## Findings

1. When one party knows more than the other, the informed party can separate itself by an action that is cheaper for a high-quality type than for a low-quality type. The signal has to be costly to imitate; the cost, not the content, carries the information [^spence-job-market-signaling].
2. The signaling-theory review defines the setting as two parties with different information, and notes the theory's prominent place in entrepreneurship research [^connelly-signaling-theory].
3. Trust is the willingness to be vulnerable to another party on the expectation that it will perform an action important to the trustor, whether or not the trustor can monitor it [^mayer-integrative-trust].
4. Trustworthiness has three components: ability (competence in a specific domain), benevolence and integrity. Each can vary independently, and trust in a party is a function of all three plus the trustor's own propensity to trust [^mayer-integrative-trust].
5. For young firms, third parties facing great uncertainty about quality rely on the prominence of a firm's affiliates to judge it. In venture-backed biotech firms, those with prominent alliance partners and equity investors reached an initial public offering faster and at higher valuations, and much of the benefit came from a transfer of status [^stuart-endorsements].
6. Among 885 institutional venture capitalists, the management team was the most-cited important factor (95% of firms) and the most important factor for 47%; business model (83%), product (74%) and market (68%) followed. Venture capitalists see the team as more important than the product or technology [^gompers-how-vcs-decide].
7. The same survey cites earlier work listing customer adoption among the factors venture capitalists consider, and 69% say they help portfolio companies connect to customers. Customer contact is therefore part of how investors both judge and build a company [^gompers-how-vcs-decide].

## Caveats

- No source studies letters of intent or sales pipelines. The Answer carries the claim from general signaling and trust theory to early sales by inference: that a sale or pursued opportunity is observable evidence of ability (Mayer, Davis and Schoorman) and that a committed buyer or letter of intent works as an endorsement (Stuart, Hoang and Hybels, who studied alliance partners and investors, not customers). No Finding states either point; they are the author's reasoning.
- The strongest counter-evidence is that investors rank team above business factors [^gompers-how-vcs-decide]. A sales result therefore persuades mainly as evidence about the team, and the survey does not rank traction on its own.
- A letter of intent is cheap for a buyer to give, and non-binding. Under signaling theory a signal that costs little to imitate separates weak from strong firms poorly [^spence-job-market-signaling]. How binding the commitment is changes how much trust it earns; this is an inference from the theory, not a tested result.
- The endorsement study is on biotechnology firms heading to an initial public offering; the status mechanism may differ for software or services [^stuart-endorsements].
- The signaling review notes that the theory's central tenets have blurred as it spread across organizational research [^connelly-signaling-theory]. Mayer, Davis and Schoorman's model was read from its abstract and a summary, not the full text, because the PDF was scanned images. Spence's paper was not read in full either; finding 1 rests on its abstract and on the signaling review's account of it.
