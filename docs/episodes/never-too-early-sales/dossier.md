# Start selling before you need the money {#start-selling-early}

## Why watching cashflow is mission critical {#why-watching-cashflow-is-mission-critical}

#### Voice script

1. **Opening promise** — a company can be fine on paper and bankrupt in March; today: why sales starts early, and four first steps.
2. **Who this is for** — founders and founding-team members, business or technical, who know they should sell but treat it as later.

### Watch your cashflow {#watch-your-cashflow}

#### Speaker notes

1. **Costs start first** — company setup and the founders' living costs arise before the first user or any income [1].
   - sources: [1] Paul Graham, A Student's Guide to Startups: Startup Funding — https://www.paulgraham.com/startupfunding.html
   - target: the cost notes in January
2. **Even a signed deal waits** — with no contract term, interest on a late B2B payment starts only 30 days after the invoice is received, and agreed terms may run up to 60 days [2].
   - sources: [2] European Parliament and Council, Directive 2011/7/EU on combating late payment in commercial transactions — https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32011L0007
   - target: the gap between the March and April columns

#### Voice script

1. **The board** — sticky notes, Jan to April, cash, cost, revenue; costs every month.
2. **Big deal lands in April** — the one big revenue note too late; cash hits zero in March; bankrupt.
3. **Find your zero-cash month** — first action: build this month-by-month view for your own company, find the month cash reaches zero.

## Why sales is more than just selling {#why-sales-is-more-than-just-selling}

#### Voice script

1. **Second reason** — cash is the first reason to start; selling early pays back before any revenue does.

### Sales is more than selling {#sales-is-more-than-selling}

#### Visual

master: grid-1x3 · motion: local

Three columns, one per thing early selling gives you: market insight, a repeatable process, and trust with investors and customers. Beneath them one line draws them together into a single result, "increase trust". The columns react only to hover.

#### Speaker notes

1. **A founder's picture of users is wrong** — a founder's initial model of users is always inaccurate, even when the founder is one of them [1].
   - sources: [1] Graham, Do Things that Don't Scale — https://paulgraham.com/ds.html
   - target: the market insight column
2. **Why early deals teach the process** — a startup is an organization built to search for a repeatable and scalable business model, so the process is found by selling, not designed in advance [2].
   - sources: [2] Blank, What's A Startup? First Principles — https://steveblank.com/2010/01/25/whats-a-startup-first-principles/
   - target: the repeatable process column
3. **Investors read the team** — among 885 venture capitalists, 95% named the management team an important factor, more than business model, product or market [3].
   - sources: [3] Gompers, Gornall, Kaplan and Strebulaev, How Do Venture Capitalists Make Decisions? — https://www.nber.org/papers/w22587
   - target: the trust column
4. **Trust needs a signal that costs something** — a signal carries information only when it is costly for a weaker firm to imitate, so a trust-building signal has to cost the sender something [4].
   - sources: [4] Spence, Job Market Signaling — https://doi.org/10.2307/1882010

#### Voice script

1. **Insight** — what you learn from real prospects replaces what you assumed.
2. **Process** — early deals closed by you show who buys, why, how long; that becomes the process.
3. **Trust** — early results such as a letter of intent or a filled pipeline show you can win buyers.
4. **All three build trust** — insight, process and results together give buyers and investors a reason to trust you, before a euro of revenue.

### Stages of sale {#stages-of-sale}

#### Visual

master: three-column · motion: local

No library match: three boxes in a left-to-right flow, "Leads", "Opportunities", "Closing", joined by arrows. Under Leads two small labels: "research" and "first contact (cold call, inbound, network)". Opportunities is drawn largest, as the real work. After Closing a short trailing arrow marks delivery, labelled as more lag before cash. The arrows draw in from left to right when the slide appears.

#### Speaker notes

1. **Delivery adds lag** — after the work is done the customer's payment period may only start after an acceptance step of up to 30 days [1].
   - sources: [1] European Parliament and Council, Directive 2011/7/EU on combating late payment in commercial transactions — https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32011L0007
   - target: the delivery arrow
2. **Early channels do not scale, and that is fine** — before product-market fit, rely on the personal networks of founders, employees and investors and on customer referrals; they yield the best opportunities to learn from [2].
   - sources: [2] Mark Roberge, The Science of Scaling — https://f.hubspotusercontent10.net/hubfs/6575667/Stage%202s%20Science%20of%20Scaling.pdf
   - target: the first contact labels

#### Voice script

1. **Three stages** — leads, opportunities, closing; left to right.
2. **Leads** — research and keep a list of potential customers; start the list now.
3. **First contact** — cold call, inbound, network; open opportunities.
4. **Opportunities** — real work lives here; conversations, proposals.
5. **After closing** — delivery, more lag before cash; revenue is later than the signature.

## Why building an effective sales pipeline takes time {#why-building-an-effective-sales-pipeline-takes-time}

#### Voice script

1. **Why not start later** — a pipeline is not a switch; three slides on what it measures, an example, and why ramp-up takes months.

### What is a sales pipeline {#what-is-a-sales-pipeline}

#### Visual

master: grid-1x3 · motion: local

Three columns: "Sales funnel" tells you about your volume, "Conversion rate" about your offering, "Sales cycle" about your efficiency. The columns react only to hover.

#### Speaker notes

1. **Know your cycle** — Blank's exit criteria for validating a sales process ask whether the sales cycle is understood, including average selling price, lifetime value and return on investment [1].
   - sources: [1] Blank, The Customer Development Methodology (course slides) — https://innovation.ucsd.edu/startup/startup-toolkit/Steve-Blank-CustDev.pdf
   - target: the sales cycle column

#### Voice script

1. **Funnel** — leads, opportunities, deals; how many enter; says volume.
2. **Conversion rate** — share that moves to next stage; says whether the offering fits.
3. **Sales cycle** — time from first contact to deal; says efficiency.
4. **Measure both** — fourth action: track deals, measure conversion rate and cycle.

### Pipeline by the numbers {#pipeline-by-the-numbers}

#### Visual

master: basic-page · motion: local

No library match: a horizontal funnel of three stages, lead, opportunity and deal, each labelled with a ratio and a duration. Lead to opportunity reads 3:1 and takes 2 weeks, opportunity to deal reads 9:1 and takes 6 months, and the deal stage takes 3 months, giving 27:1 in total, over a cycle of about 9.5 months. A caption under the funnel reads "Illustration, not a benchmark". The stages fill in one after the other as the slide appears.

#### Speaker notes

1. **A real dataset, other numbers** — in one vendor dataset of 655 thousand opportunities, the new-customer win rate was 19 percent (about 5.3 opportunities per win) with a 91-day sales cycle and 8 stakeholders per deal, against 45 percent, 52 days and 5 stakeholders for expansion sales [1].
   - sources: [1] Ebsta and Pavilion, GTM Benchmarks Report — https://benchmarks.ebsta.com/hubfs/V3%202025%20Benchmark%20Report/gtm_benchmarks_digital_report.pdf
   - target: the 27:1 ratio
2. **The biggest loss is no decision** — 40 to 60 percent of deals are lost to buyers who intend to buy but never act [2].
   - sources: [2] Dixon and McKenna, Stop Losing Sales to Customer Indecision (Harvard Business Review) — https://hbr.org/2022/06/stop-losing-sales-to-customer-indecision
3. **Why cycles are long** — decision authority rests with a group whose members have different roles and all have veto power [3].
   - sources: [3] Schmidt, Adamson and Bird, Making the Consensus Sale (Harvard Business Review) — https://hbr.org/2015/03/making-the-consensus-sale
   - target: the duration labels

#### Voice script

1. **Walk the funnel** — lead to opportunity 3 to 1; opportunity to deal 9 to 1; 27 to 1 overall.
2. **Fill it with many** — many more leads than deals; plan the list size backwards from the deals you need.
3. **Time adds up** — cycle about nine and a half months in this example; long cycles push revenue out.
4. **Illustration only** — your numbers will differ; measure your own conversion rate and cycle.

### Why it takes time to ramp up {#why-it-takes-time-to-ramp-up}

#### Visual

master: grid-3x1 · motion: local

Three rows in reading order: "Preparation", "Unknown market", "Unknown metrics", each with a short caption. The rows react only to hover.

#### Speaker notes

1. **Metrics lag by one cycle** — recent leads look worse than the long-run value because they have not yet gone through a full cycle [1].
   - sources: [1] Mark Roberge, The Science of Scaling — https://f.hubspotusercontent10.net/hubfs/6575667/Stage%202s%20Science%20of%20Scaling.pdf
   - target: the unknown metrics row
2. **Scaling too early burns cash** — premature scaling of sales and marketing is the leading cause of cash loss in a startup [2].
   - sources: [2] Steve Blank, It Must Be A Marketing Problem — https://steveblank.com/2010/02/11/it-must-be-a-marketing-problem/
   - target: the preparation row
3. **Customers do not come to you** — "You can't wait for users to come to you. You have to go out and get them." [3].
   - sources: [3] Paul Graham, Do Things that Don't Scale — https://paulgraham.com/ds.html
   - target: the unknown market row

#### Voice script

1. **Preparation** — write down a process before you can repeat it.
2. **Unknown market** — who buys, why, what price; only learned in conversations.
3. **Unknown metrics** — you do not yet know your own conversion rate or cycle length; the first numbers are guesses.
4. **So start early** — every month of delay is a month of learning lost.
