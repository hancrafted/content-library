#### Speaker notes

1. **Recognition has its own trigger** — IFRS 15 ties revenue recognition to satisfying a performance obligation through transfer of a good or service. That accounting trigger is not the receipt of cash; the board is deliberately not an income statement [1].
   - sources: [1] European Commission, Regulation (EU) 2016/1905 (endorsing IFRS 15 Revenue from Contracts with Customers) — https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32016R1905
2. **Expenses and payments can differ too** — Publication 538 distinguishes incurred expenses under accrual accounting from paid expenses under cash accounting. It describes US tax accounting, not a universal financial-reporting rule; this example explicitly assumes costs are paid monthly [2].
   - sources: [2] Internal Revenue Service, Publication 538: Accounting Periods and Methods — https://www.irs.gov/publications/p538

### Sales is more than selling {#sales-is-more-than-selling}

#### Visual

#### Speaker notes

1. **Two different intention results** — Murphy et al.'s stated-preference meta-analysis found a median hypothetical-to-actual willingness-to-pay ratio of 1.35 across 83 observations from 28 studies, with substantial skew. Morwitz et al. found weaker intention–purchase correlation for new than existing products. Neither result gives a correction factor for a B2B prospect or proves that all purchase intentions exaggerate demand [1] [2].
   - sources: [1] Murphy et al., A Meta-analysis of Hypothetical Bias in Stated Preference Valuation — https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10640-004-3332-z
   - sources: [2] Morwitz, Steckel and Gupta, When do purchase intentions predict sales? — https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijforecast.2007.05.015
2. **History has counter-evidence** — Poppo, Zhou and Ryu report that continuity expectations mediate the positive history–trust relationship; with weak continuity expectations, longer history was associated with lower trust. Doney and Cannon found no effect of trust on current supplier selection after controlling for previous experience and performance, although trust related to anticipated future interaction. These abstract-level findings are not proof that early startup outreach causes trust or purchases [3] [4].
   - sources: [3] Poppo, Zhou and Ryu, Alternative Origins to Interorganizational Trust: An Interdependence Perspective on the Shadow of the Past and the Shadow of the Future — https://doi.org/10.1287/orsc.1070.0281
   - sources: [4] Doney and Cannon, An Examination of the Nature of Trust in Buyer-Seller Relationships — https://doi.org/10.2307/1251829
3. **Commitment is not trust** — Anderson and Weitz studied idiosyncratic investments and commitment in 378 manufacturer–distributor pairs. That is evidence about commitment, not a measurement of trust; these concepts must not be interchanged [5].
   - sources: [5] Anderson and Weitz, The Use of Pledges to Build and Sustain Commitment in Distribution Channels — https://doi.org/10.2307/3172490
4. **What the internal-learning trial tested** — Both groups of 116 Italian startups received feedback training; the treatment group additionally learned predictive frameworks and rigorous hypothesis tests, performed better and pivoted more. The trial did not isolate sales contact, measure capacity limits or compare feedback with no feedback [6].
   - sources: [6] Camuffo et al., A Scientific Approach to Entrepreneurial Decision Making: Evidence from a Randomized Control Trial — https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2018.3249

#### Voice script

1. **Test an offer, not applause** — MARKET insight; concrete problem; proposed price; requested next step; enthusiasm versus observed response
2. **Learn from a no** — OBJECTION notes; wrong buyer, timing, unclear value; questions rather than instant verdict about whole market
3. **Early buyers are a small sample** — SELF-SELECTION; network bias; compare conversations; avoid building everything for one enthusiastic prospect
4. **Give reasons to keep talking** — TRUST can develop; realistic promises; follow-through; practical recommendation, not causal guarantee; outreach alone insufficient
5. **Test what you can deliver** — INTERNAL insight; offer promise versus actual work; log delivery uncertainty; capacity exposure as hypothesis, not measured research result
6. **Turn contact into a learning loop** — ASSUMPTION; buyer response; next test; learn without treating every change as progress; revenue remains necessary

#### Speaker notes

#### Speaker notes

1. **Tenure is not a stopwatch for ramp-up** — Peasley and Hochstein's authors' summary discusses early-tenure salespeople hired 18–24 months before the survey; that tenure and their framing do not measure a founder's pipeline-from-zero ramp period. The source also reports a prospecting-versus-account-work trade-off, not that maximum prospecting is always best [1].
   - sources: [1] Peasley and Hochstein, Insights for Optimizing Early-Tenure Sales Success (summary of their Journal of Personal Selling & Sales Management article, doi:10.1080/08853134.2024.2316383) — https://kellercenter.hankamer.baylor.edu/news/story/2025/insights-optimizing-early-tenure-sales-success
2. **Training evidence has a narrow setting** — Chung, Kim and Park fit a structural model to one firm's sales force; tenure and training affected modelled performance, with stronger training effects for junior staff. That does not establish how long a startup founder needs to become productive [2].
   - sources: [2] Chung, Kim and Park, The Comprehensive Effects of Sales Force Management: A Dynamic Structural Analysis of Selection, Compensation, and Training — https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/SF_Management_2020_0430_Final_1866af08-e5ee-4b12-9d51-7e3074d47c5d.pdf
3. **Budget association is not a timing prescription** — The longitudinal B2B high-tech startup survey associates a larger personal-sales budget share with better early-stage performance and worse later-stage performance. It does not demonstrate that a budget shift causes faster ramp-up; this result was available at abstract level [3].
   - sources: [3] Vomberg et al., The Different Effects of Mass-Media Marketing and Personal Sales Budgets Across the Life Cycle of B2B High-Tech Start-Ups — https://doi.org/10.1177/00222437251367766
4. **Buyer-led journeys are conceptual evidence** — Kalwey et al.'s interview-based conceptual paper describes a role coordinating buyer–seller touchpoints. It does not quantify sales-cycle duration or prove a combined duration for construction, learning and buyer pacing [4].
   - sources: [4] Kalwey et al., Holistic Selling: An Emerging Paradigm in B2B Markets — https://doi.org/10.1177/00222429251338820

#### Speaker notes

1. **An unpaid earned amount is not spendable cash** — IFRS 15 distinguishes contract assets and receivables after transfer from cash settlement. A receivable can record a right to payment while the account still lacks the funds needed for an outgoing payment [1].
