# Why AI relocates the pressure, and why requirements come first

## Format

Explorable page, slug `software-ecology`, caption "What the customer really wanted: the AI edition". Three Slides: the Title slide, a Play slide holding the whole Explorable with its toolbar, and one Slide explaining why, how and what.

## Audience

Both Audiences, leaning Organization. Managers know their org chart but not how AI pressure moves through it; they want a top-down view of how AI affects their organization. Individuals know their own task and see it as the essential one; they want to zoom out to a systems thinking view and see what is adjacent to them.

## Style

Editorial: managers are the lead audience, and its meaning colours (`human`, `machine`, `ai`) map onto a node's human and technical sides and the AI pump.

## Learning goal

The viewer acquires a systems thinking perspective: they can see where AI will increase the pressure in their own organization, and why clear requirements are the key to reducing it.

## Actionables

1. Map your own team as nodes and handoffs, and mark the narrowest review step.
2. Before you speed up a node with AI, check that the review step after it can handle more.
3. Before you hand a task to AI, write the requirement clearly enough that someone new could act on it.

## Hook

You all know this cartoon. With AI, the wrong swing gets built ten times faster.

## Red thread

The cartoon was never about bad actors, it's about the system: a game of telephone translating an intent humans never voiced well. Unclear requirements always led to bad results, and AI just accelerates everything: it pushes the pressure into specification and review, so the clearest requirements are what lower it.

## Analogy

The organization is plumbing. Roles are stretches of pipe, handoffs are the pipes between them, and review is the narrow valve. AI is the pump that moves whatever you feed it, faster. Clear requirements are clean water; context rot is sediment on the pipe walls.

## Constraints

- AI is always shown as an accelerator, never as a solution.
- No bad actors: no role is blamed, the pressure belongs to the system.
- Every node shows both its human side and its technical side.
- Reusable across talks: no dated claims and no tool names.
