# Will AI reduce your work? It adds work: managing it. {#will-ai-reduce-your-work}

## From execution to management {#from-execution-to-management}

#### Voice script

1. **The question** — people ask: will AI reduce my work, speed it up; picture a colleague asking you this over coffee
2. **The reframe** — faulty premise; AI adds work, because you can now do far more complex tasks; managing them is the work it adds
3. **The payoff** — you accomplish much more, if you upskill and learn to manage AI like a team; today is the chance

### Every revolution, one card {#every-revolution-one-card}

#### Speaker notes

1. **A cheaper unit does not mean less total work** — Jevons argued Watt's inventions economised heat and that further improvement would accelerate coal use; separately, in Scotland coal per ton of iron fell to under a third while total consumption rose about tenfold [1].
   - sources: [1] Jevons, The Coal Question (1865) — https://www.econlib.org/library/YPDBooks/Jevons/jvnCQ.html
2. **The tool lifts the tasks left to people** — when ATMs quadrupled, tellers per branch fell by more than a third, yet branches rose by more than 40 percent and tellers moved towards relationship banking [2].
   - sources: [2] Autor, Why Are There Still So Many Jobs? The History and Future of Workplace Automation — https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/jep.29.3.3
   - target: the lower layer of the steam engine and computer cards
3. **The plough is the weaker card** — Boserup finds the plough often prevented a fall in output per man-hour rather than raised it, and the extra work came from cropping land more often [4].
   - sources: [4] Boserup, The Conditions of Agricultural Growth — https://www.biw.kuleuven.be/aee/clo/idessa_files/boserup1965.pdf
   - target: the farming card
4. **Some workers did rise, by several routes** — a contemporary defender of the factory system claimed regular, skilled workers could advance to overlooker, manager or partner, and 18 percent of a sample of British mechanics and engineers became hired engineers and managers; but owners found managers by many routes, and no source measures how many came from the shop floor [1][2].
   - sources: [1] Ure, The Philosophy of Manufactures (1835) — https://archive.org/details/philosophyofmanu00urea
   - sources: [2] Meisenzahl and Mokyr, The Rate and Direction of Invention in the British Industrial Revolution: Incentives and Institutions — https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w16993/w16993.pdf
   - target: the fork under the steam engine card
5. **Steam shifted a region's skill mix** — English counties that used more steam engines had a larger share of skilled workers; no study found follows individual adopters rising, and schooling and wage evidence for the period is mixed [1].
   - sources: [1] de Pleijt, Nuvolari and Weisdorf, Human Capital Formation During the First Industrial Revolution: Evidence from the use of Steam Engines — https://doi.org/10.1093/jeea/jvz006
   - target: the fork under the steam engine card
6. **AI completes longer tasks on its own** — the task length a frontier model completes at 50 percent success was about 50 minutes in early 2025 and has doubled roughly every seven months since 2019 [1].
   - sources: [1] Kwa et al., Measuring AI Ability to Complete Long Tasks (METR) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14499
   - target: the AI card
7. **The gain is real but conditional** — 758 BCG consultants using AI finished 12.2 percent more tasks, 25.1 percent faster, at over 40 percent higher quality inside the model's frontier, and were 19 percentage points less likely to be correct on a task outside it; a trial with 16 experienced developers found a 19 percent slowdown with early-2025 tools [3][5].
   - sources: [3] Dell'Acqua et al., Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier, HBS Working Paper 24-013 (SSRN 4573321) — https://mitsloan.mit.edu/sites/default/files/2023-10/SSRN-id4573321.pdf
   - sources: [5] Becker, Rush, Barnes, Rein, Measuring the Impact of Early-2025 AI on Experienced Open-Source Developer Productivity — https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.09089
   - target: the AI card
8. **The same prompt can answer differently** — across five models and eight tasks run ten times with settings meant to be deterministic, accuracy varied by up to 15 percent between runs, unlike a plough or a steam engine [3].
   - sources: [3] Atil et al., LLM Stability: A detailed analysis with some surprises — https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.04667
   - target: the AI card

#### Voice script

1. **Every revolution, one card** — plough, steam engine, computer; machine takes the predictable work, people move to judging, deciding, coordinating
2. **The climb** — lower layer card by card; farmer plans the harvest, worker plans the line; each time you move up
3. **The fork, no judgment** — some who took up managing the machines moved up, others stayed on the line, nothing wrong with that; seismic shifts open the door
4. **Now it thinks** — first tool that does part of the unpredictable work itself: language, reasoning, creativity, brainstorming; you manage something that thinks
5. **Biggest step** — one person does tasks that needed a whole team; AI card biggest; fork left open, my hypothesis it opens again

### You were promoted {#you-were-promoted}

#### Visual

master: horizontal-rail · motion: local

A person standing in an "execution" box on the left and a "management" box on the right, with the gap between them labelled "the misalignment". Progressive disclosure fills the gap with four stepping stones in order (break it down, write clear goals, review, delegate); the last reveal names the gap the language barrier.

#### Speaker notes

1. **Many skip the management steps** — in 9,830 Claude.ai conversations, 51.1 percent stated a goal, 15.8 percent questioned the reasoning and 8.7 percent checked facts; no study measures executing versus managing, so the research supports "many", not "most" [1].
   - sources: [1] Anthropic, The AI Fluency Index (February 2026) — https://academy.claude.com/tutorials/the-ai-fluency-index
   - target: the gap labelled the misalignment
2. **Workers describe the shift themselves** — in a survey of 319 weekly GenAI users, effort moved from executing tasks to stewarding them and from gathering information to verifying it, while higher confidence in the AI went with less checking [5].
   - sources: [5] Lee et al., The Impact of Generative AI on Critical Thinking: Self-Reported Reductions in Cognitive Effort and Confidence Effects From a Survey of Knowledge Workers (CHI 2025) — https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/uploads/prod/2025/01/lee_2025_ai_critical_thinking_survey.pdf
3. **Stepping stone: break it down** — users split complex tasks into smaller steps, which creates a feedback loop where the model corrects course [2].
   - sources: [2] Anthropic, Economic Index: New building blocks for AI use (January 2026) — https://www.anthropic.com/research/economic-index-primitives
   - target: the first stepping stone
4. **Pocock's words are "communication barrier"** — he says there is a communication barrier between a person and an AI agent, located in vocabulary: they do not share the same language, so you need a shared language; the phrase "language barrier" is not his, and the management reading is this project's inference [1].
   - sources: [1] Matt Pocock, Software Fundamentals Matter More Than Ever (AI Engineer Europe 2026) — https://ai.engineer/talks/v4F1gFy-hqg-software-fundamentals-for-ai-coding
   - target: the last reveal

#### Voice script

1. **You were promoted** — AI moves you from doing to managing; the work does not shrink, it changes
2. **The misalignment** — biggest one today; many of us still act as if we do the work, not manage it
3. **Four stepping stones** — break it down, clear goals, review, delegate; you already know them from people
4. **The barrier** — you and the AI do not share a vocabulary; naming the gap is the first step across it

### Practise it today, at work {#practise-it-today-at-work}

#### Visual

master: grid-3x1 · motion: local

Three rows, each pairing a moment at work with its AI equivalent. At rest only the work side shows; each click reveals the AI side. Row one: hand off work to a colleague, or before a vacation, with a proper handoff and enough context, mirrors starting a new chat with that context. Row two: onboard a new team member mirrors opening a new AI session. Row three: break down a big project into chunks, with the seams between them defined so each task feeds the overall goal, mirrors splitting work into AI tasks. The closing line "more complex projects are now within reach" sits under the rows.

#### Speaker notes

1. **Briefing, context and checking are the discipline** — Dell'Acqua [3] finds AI helps less experienced people on bounded tasks inside the model's competence; that effective delegation rests on briefing, supplying context and verifying is this project's inference from the research file, not a finding of the study.
   - sources: [3] Dell'Acqua et al., Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier, HBS Working Paper 24-013, September 2023 (SSRN 4573321) — https://mitsloan.mit.edu/sites/default/files/2023-10/SSRN-id4573321.pdf
   - target: all three rows
2. **A new AI session is a new contributor** — experienced developers described AI as a new contributor lacking tacit knowledge of the repository, which is hard to hand over as context, and spent about 9 percent of their time reviewing and cleaning its output [7].
   - sources: [7] Becker, Rush, Barnes, Rein, Measuring the Impact of Early-2025 AI on Experienced Open-Source Developer Productivity — https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.09089
   - target: the onboarding row
3. **Experience with the tool predicts results** — users with six months or more of tenure show a success rate about 3 to 5 points higher within the same task cluster and delegate less by directive; Anthropic notes learning by doing fits the data but is not proven [8].
   - sources: [8] Anthropic, Anthropic Economic Index report: Learning Curves (March 2026) — https://www.anthropic.com/research/economic-index-march-2026-report
   - target: the closing line
4. **Splitting tasks lengthens what you can finish** — users split tasks into steps and pick tasks they expect to work, which accounts for part of the long task lengths seen in practice [9].
   - sources: [9] Anthropic, Economic Index: New building blocks for AI use (January 2026) — https://www.anthropic.com/research/economic-index-primitives
   - target: the break-down row

#### Voice script

1. **Practise on people** — these skills work on colleagues today, so people skills become AI skills
2. **The handoff** — before a vacation, or any handoff: proper handoff with enough context; same as a new chat with context
3. **The new teammate** — onboarding a new team member is opening a new AI session; nothing carries over unless you say it
4. **Chunks and seams** — big project: smaller chunks, define the seams between them so each task feeds the overall goal
5. **An invitation** — more complex projects now within reach; not a burden, a chance to grow; which row will you start with
