# Start selling before you need the money {#start-selling-before-you-need-the-money}

## Why watching cashflow is mission critical {#why-watching-cashflow-is-mission-critical}

#### Voice script

1. **Hook** — A company that goes bankrupt in March while its biggest deal lands in April. Same numbers, one month off.
2. **Today's route** — Four steps through the talk: see your cash, build a pipeline, track it, start it early.

### Watch your cashflow {#watch-your-cashflow}

#### Visual

master: basic-page · motion: local

Design reference: reference/source-deck/design-reference.md#page-3

A planning board with one column per month, January to April, as yellow cash notes, red cost notes and a green revenue note. January shows cash of $100,000 and a cost of $50,000, February cash of $50,000 and a cost of $50,000, and the cash note then reads $0. March carries only a cost note. April carries a revenue note of $1,000,000. The notes appear month by month, left to right. When it reaches March, "Bankrupt" is stamped across the board and April's revenue note stays out of reach.

#### Speaker notes

1. **Costs come first** — a startup's first expenses, such as company setup and the founders' living costs, arise before it has users or income [2].
   - sources: [2] Paul Graham, A Student's Guide to Startups: Startup Funding — https://www.paulgraham.com/startupfunding.html
   - target: the cost notes in the visual
2. **Promised money arrives late** — closing an investor takes longer than expected even after a yes, and one angel took months to pay [2].
   - sources: [2] Paul Graham, A Student's Guide to Startups: Startup Funding — https://www.paulgraham.com/startupfunding.html
3. **Hiring ahead does not pay itself** — there is little connection between how much a startup spends and how fast it grows, so rising costs do not bring revenue forward [1].
   - sources: [1] Paul Graham, Default Alive or Default Dead? — https://paulgraham.com/aord.html

#### Voice script

1. **Read the board** — Cash 100, cost 50 a month, revenue only in April. Walk it month by month.
2. **Zero-cash month** — Cash hits zero at the end of February. March cannot be paid. That month is the number to find.
3. **First actionable** — Build this view for your own company: month by month, cash, cost, revenue. Mark your zero-cash month.
4. **Right deal, wrong month** — The revenue is real. It is simply late. Timing decides, not the total.

## Why sales is more than just selling {#why-sales-is-more-than-just-selling}

#### Voice script

1. **Sales pays before revenue** — Selling early pays you back long before the first invoice is paid.

### Sales is more than selling {#sales-is-more-than-selling}

#### Visual

master: basic-page · motion: local

Design reference: reference/source-deck/design-reference.md#page-4

Three sticky notes on the board, "Market insight", "Trust" and "Repeatable process", each with a short line of what it brings. Arrows run from each note into one note on the right, "Increase trust". The arrows draw in one after the other.

#### Speaker notes

1. **Your guess of the customer is wrong** — a founder's initial model of users is always inaccurate, even if the founder is one of them; direct contact is what corrects it [1].
   - sources: [1] Graham, Do Things that Don't Scale — https://paulgraham.com/ds.html
   - target: the "Market insight" note
2. **Investors read the team** — among 885 venture capitalists the management team was the most-cited important factor (95%), ahead of product and market. That a signed letter of intent or a full pipeline raises trust is an inference: no study tests them directly [5].
   - sources: [5] Gompers, Gornall, Kaplan and Strebulaev, How Do Venture Capitalists Make Decisions? — https://www.nber.org/papers/w22587
   - target: the "Trust" note
3. **Scale after you find the process** — premature scaling of sales and marketing is the leading cause of cash loss in a startup; scale after the founders have found a repeatable sales model [2].
   - sources: [2] Blank, It Must Be A Marketing Problem — https://steveblank.com/2010/02/11/it-must-be-a-marketing-problem/
   - target: the "Repeatable process" note

#### Voice script

1. **Market insight** — Every conversation with a prospect replaces a guess with a fact. You learn who buys and what matters.
2. **Trust** — Investors and customers trust a company that has already sold. An early result is proof someone paid attention or money.
3. **Repeatable process** — Founders who sell learn which steps work and in which order. That is the process you can later hand to a first sales hire.
4. **Converge on trust** — Three gains, one effect: a company others dare to bet on.

## How to start doing sales {#how-to-start-doing-sales}

#### Voice script

1. **From idea to first step** — Now the practical part: what the stages of a sale are, and what you do first.

### Stages of sale {#stages-of-sale}

#### Visual

master: three-column · motion: local

Design reference: reference/source-deck/design-reference.md#page-6, reference/source-deck/design-reference.md#page-7

Three sticky-note columns on the board, read left to right and linked by arrows: "Leads", "Opportunities" and "Closing". Under Leads sit two notes, "Research and maintain a list of potential customers" and "First contact: cold call, inbound, network". Under Opportunities sit "Follow-up, sales material, meetings, negotiations". Under Closing sits "Customer agrees to a contract". A fourth, fainter note after Closing reads "Delivery, then payment", to show the cash arrives later still. The columns appear one after the other.

#### Speaker notes

1. **Delivery is not payment** — with no contract term, interest on a late B2B payment starts only 30 days after the invoice is received, and contract terms may run up to 60 days [3].
   - sources: [3] European Parliament and Council, Directive 2011/7/EU on combating late payment in commercial transactions — https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32011L0007
   - target: the "Delivery, then payment" note
2. **Acceptance adds time** — an acceptance or verification step can add up to 30 more days before the payment period starts [3].
   - sources: [3] European Parliament and Council, Directive 2011/7/EU on combating late payment in commercial transactions — https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32011L0007

#### Voice script

1. **Three stages** — Leads, opportunities, closing. The path every customer walks.
2. **Second actionable, lead list** — Start lead generation now. Research and keep a list of potential customers.
3. **Third actionable, first contact** — Make first contact: cold call, inbound lead, your network. Contact opens opportunities.
4. **Opportunities are the work** — Interest is only the start. Follow-up, material, meetings, negotiation.
5. **Lag after the deal** — Even a closed deal pays later: delivery, invoice, payment terms.

1. **One board for all deals** — Every deal is one note in one column. Stage, owner and value visible at a glance.
2. **Fourth actionable, track deals** — Track every deal in a simple pipeline. A spreadsheet or a free tool is enough to start.
3. **Move or drop** — A deal that does not move gets a decision, not a hope.

#### Speaker notes

1. **Customers do not arrive on their own** — "You can't wait for users to come to you. You have to go out and get them"; getting users is a gradual process [3].

#### Voice script

1. **Volume** — Marketing publishes to many people. Some raise a hand.
2. **Enter a defined stage** — A person who raises a hand lands in one pipeline stage, for example an inbound lead. Sales then makes contact.
3. **You do not need it first** — Network and cold calls start before any marketing exists.

## Why building an effective sales pipeline takes time {#why-building-an-effective-sales-pipeline-takes-time}

#### Voice script

1. **Not a switch** — A pipeline is not a switch you flip when the money runs low. It is built, measured and tuned.

### What is a sales pipeline {#what-is-a-sales-pipeline}

#### Visual

master: grid-1x3 · motion: local

Design reference: reference/source-deck/design-reference.md#page-10

Three sticky notes on the board, each a number the company watches: "Sales funnel" with the line "volume per stage", "Conversion rate" with "attractiveness of your offering" and "Sales cycle" with "efficiency". The funnel note is the one marked as primary.

#### Speaker notes

1. **Rates differ per stage** — in one dataset the MQL-to-SQL rate was 68 percent beside a 19 percent win rate [4]. Inference, not a finding: stage definitions may differ per company, so rates may not be comparable across companies.
   - sources: [4] Ebsta and Pavilion, GTM Benchmarks Report — https://benchmarks.ebsta.com/hubfs/V3%202025%20Benchmark%20Report/gtm_benchmarks_digital_report.pdf
   - target: the "Conversion rate" note

#### Voice script

1. **Funnel** — Each stage of the sale, and how much volume sits in each.
2. **Conversion rate** — How many in one stage become one in the next. It says how attractive your offering is.
3. **Sales cycle** — Days from first contact to closed deal or paid invoice. It says how efficient you are.
4. **Fourth actionable, measure** — Measure your conversion rate and your sales cycle from your first deals on.

### Pipeline by the numbers {#pipeline-by-the-numbers}

#### Visual

master: basic-page · motion: local

Design reference: reference/source-deck/design-reference.md#page-11

Our own drawn funnel of three stages, Lead, Opportunity and Deal. Between Lead and Opportunity a note reads 3:1, between Opportunity and Deal 9:1, with a total of 27:1 below. Under each stage a duration: 2 weeks, 6 months, 3 months, adding to a 9.5-month cycle. A label on the whole picture reads "Illustration of a complex sale, not a benchmark". Beside it sits a smaller note with measured figures from one vendor dataset: 19% new-customer win rate and a 91-day sales cycle. The stages draw in left to right and the durations add up on a bar below.

#### Speaker notes

1. **Measured, one dataset** — in a dataset of 655 thousand opportunities across 387 companies, the new-customer win rate was 19 percent, the sales cycle 91 days and 8 stakeholders per deal; for expansion sales to existing customers it was 45 percent, 52 days and 5 stakeholders [4].
   - sources: [4] Ebsta and Pavilion, GTM Benchmarks Report — https://benchmarks.ebsta.com/hubfs/V3%202025%20Benchmark%20Report/gtm_benchmarks_digital_report.pdf
   - target: the measured note beside the funnel
2. **Most losses are no decision** — an analysis of more than 2.5 million sales conversations found that 40 to 60 percent of deals are lost to buyers who intend to buy but never act [1].
   - sources: [1] Dixon and McKenna, Stop Losing Sales to Customer Indecision (Harvard Business Review) — https://hbr.org/2022/06/stop-losing-sales-to-customer-indecision
3. **The buyer is a group** — a Gartner survey of 632 B2B buyers describes buying groups of five to 16 people, and 74 percent of buyer teams showed unhealthy conflict [3].
   - sources: [3] Gartner, Gartner Sales Survey Finds 74% of B2B Buyer Teams Demonstrate "Unhealthy Conflict" During The Decision Process — https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-05-07-gartner-sales-survey-finds-74-percent-of-b2b-buyer-teams-demonstrate-unhealthy-conflict-during-the-decision-process

#### Voice script

1. **Illustration** — An example of a complex sale. Not a benchmark; yours will differ.
2. **Ratios multiply** — Three leads to one opportunity, nine opportunities to one deal: twenty-seven leads for one deal.
3. **Time adds up** — Two weeks, six months, three months. Revenue lands most of a year after the first contact.
4. **Measured sits beside it** — A real dataset shows under one in five won and about three months. Your own numbers will tell you more.
5. **So fill early** — Many leads in, long wait out. Start filling now.

### Why it takes time to ramp up {#why-it-takes-time-to-ramp-up}

#### Visual

master: grid-1x3 · motion: local

Design reference: reference/source-deck/design-reference.md#page-15

Three sticky notes on the board: "Preparation" (sales deck, pricing, offering, business model; demo, meeting slides, proposal; contract, compliance, legal), "Unknown market" (who is your customer, what will they pay, what problem needs solving) and "Unknown metrics" (conversion rate and sales cycle, needed for backwards planning). The notes appear one after the other.

#### Speaker notes

1. **You search before you execute** — a startup is a temporary organization designed to search for a repeatable and scalable business model; the facts a pipeline depends on are found in conversations with customers [2].
   - sources: [2] Steve Blank, The Customer Development Manifesto — https://steveblank.com/category/customer-development-manifesto/
   - target: the "Unknown market" note
2. **Metrics lag by the cycle** — a conversion figure for recent leads looks worse than the long-run value because those leads have not yet gone through a full sales cycle [4].
   - sources: [4] Mark Roberge, The Science of Scaling — https://f.hubspotusercontent10.net/hubfs/6575667/Stage%202s%20Science%20of%20Scaling.pdf
   - target: the "Unknown metrics" note
3. **Codify after you learn** — Roberge advises codifying the go-to-market playbook only after the learning, and proving scalable unit economics before aggressively hiring salespeople [4].
   - sources: [4] Mark Roberge, The Science of Scaling — https://f.hubspotusercontent10.net/hubfs/6575667/Stage%202s%20Science%20of%20Scaling.pdf
   - target: the "Preparation" note

#### Voice script

1. **Preparation** — Deck, pricing, demo, contract. Written before the first deal can close.
2. **Unknown market** — Who buys, at what price, for which problem. Only conversations answer it.
3. **Unknown metrics** — Conversion and cycle are unknown at the start, and you need them to plan backwards from cash.
4. **No shortcut** — Each of the three takes real time. It cannot be rushed once the money is short.

### Watch your cashflow, "Start when?" {#watch-your-cashflow-start-when}

#### Visual

#### Speaker notes

2. **Learning goal** — Sales must start long before revenue is needed, because revenue trails costs.
3. **Your four steps** — Build the cashflow view and find your zero-cash month. Keep a lead list. Make first contact. Track deals and measure conversion and cycle.
4. **This week** — Pick the first one and do it this week.
