# Stop prompting AI, start onboarding it {#onboard-ai-like-a-colleague}

Become twenty times more productive with AI. You have read that line. Hold it up against your own screen.

## The gap {#the-gap}

First, the distance between the promise and your day.

### 20x on the poster, not on your screen {#twenty-x-versus-your-screen}

Twenty times. It is in every feed. It is in the title of every course. Your week, they say, shrinks to a single afternoon.

Now think of your own attempt. You opened the chat. You typed a prompt, a good one, you thought. The answer came back flat. Generic. Fine, but not what you needed. You fixed it by hand, and you shrugged.

So where are the twenty times? Why does it work on the poster and not for you? Hold that question. Do not answer it yet. Let it sit in the room for a moment, because we will come at it through a different door.

## The thought experiment {#thought-experiment}

A small experiment. You are the one in it.

### Sommerfest, due by five {#sommerfest-due-by-five}

It is Monday morning. You must plan the company Sommerfest. Or the Christmas party, pick your season. About a hundred people. A fully structured plan has to be on your team lead's desk, or the CEO's, by end of day.

And help has arrived. A new colleague, on their very first day. They know essentially the entire written world. Every book on event planning, every review of every venue. They are extremely confident. And eager to please. They will say yes to anything.

Take a second. Really. The clock is running, and they are standing at your desk. What do you say?

### What you would say first {#what-you-would-say-first}

You probably do not start with a task. You start with a story. Why are we throwing this party at all? Last year people left early, and this year the team needs to feel like a team again.

Then you tell them what they cannot possibly know. We have a modest budget, so it will not be the fancy hotel. We are a hundred people, not a hundred and fifty. We are a casual company, nobody wants a speech and a tie. And last year was a barbecue, and it rained.

Then you open some doors. Here is the booking system, here is your login. These are the caterers we trust, here are their numbers. And here is the shared calendar, so you can see who is away.

And you say what you want back. One structured plan, on my desk by five. Date, venue, food, money, the order of the evening.

Before they run off, you stop. And you hear yourself. Nobody told you to say any of that. It just came out, in your own words. Hold on to those words.

### Four o'clock, the plan comes back {#four-oclock-the-plan-comes-back}

It is four o'clock. The plan comes back, early. It is polished. Venue, menu, timeline, even a playlist. And it is written with total confidence.

You start reading. And one line is off. The venue it picked is already booked that weekend. Or the budget has one zero too many. It does not say maybe. It says it in the same calm voice as everything else.

So now it is on you. If you read with a pen in your hand, you catch it. If you skim, because it all looks so sure of itself, it goes straight to the CEO. The confidence sounds the same whether the line is right or wrong. Only your eye can tell the two apart.

## From doing to managing {#from-doing-to-managing}

Now look at what you just did.

### You managed it, you did not do it {#you-managed-it}

Look back at your afternoon. Did you plan the party? No. You never booked a venue. You never called a caterer. You set someone up to do it, and then you read their work.

You did not do the task. You managed it. That shift, from doing to managing, is what working with AI asks of you. It is a skill of its own, and it is not the skill that makes you good at the task itself. And it answers your flat result from the start. You prompted it like a tool, when it needed onboarding like a colleague.

This is not about rank. You do not need a title for it. The task asks for it. Experience tends to help, because older hands have managed before. But the job is waiting for everyone, from the very first prompt.

### Four disciplines, one line each {#four-disciplines}

That skill has names now. Four of them. Prompt engineering. Context engineering. Harness engineering. Graph engineering.

Prompt engineering is how you phrase the ask, as a clear brief nobody could misread. Context engineering is the background you hand over: the budget, the headcount, the culture, the past events.

Harness engineering is the tools and the environment you give access to: the booking system, the caterer contacts, the calendar. Graph engineering is managing the workflow across several agents. Project management, for AI.

Graph engineering is the newest of the four. The term has only been around since the middle of this year, and people still argue about exactly what it covers. Treat it as new, and unsettled.

### The same wall, one level up {#same-wall-one-level-up}

And it is not only you. A team hits the same wall. So does a whole company. Everyone gets the same new colleague, and nobody hands them a day-one note.

One large survey of about six thousand executives found that most firms use AI, yet most see no effect on their output so far. Leaders asked about it themselves sound more upbeat. Nobody knows the true share. The tools came in, and the way of working stayed exactly as it was.

So the value does not arrive by itself. It arrives when you reinvest. The effort AI saves you goes back in, as new skills and new disciplines. For you. For your team. Across the whole organization. AI does not remove the work. It moves it.

## Your move {#your-move}

One small habit, and one twist.

### Write the day-one note first {#write-the-day-one-note}

So here is your move. Next time you reach for AI on something real, stop. Before you type a single word.

Take a piece of paper, or an empty note. And write down what you would tell a brand-new colleague on their first day.

Four short lines. The context. The tools they would need. The brief. And how you will check their work.

It takes two minutes. Keep it small. Then, and only then, type.

### AI can help you onboard AI {#ai-can-help-you-onboard-ai}

I know. It feels like a lot of work. Context, tools, briefs, checks, and you have not even started.

But here is the twist. AI can help you onboard AI.

Let it interview you. Ask it to put the questions to you, one at a time, until your day-one note is complete. That is the grill-me approach. You answer, it asks, and then you are done.
