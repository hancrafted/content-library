# Why you must onboard AI like a new colleague

## Format

Foundations. First theory block of the "Intro to Agentic Workflows" workshop, presentational style. A standalone piece, reusable across many AI talks as the cornerstone reference for "treat working with AI like onboarding a capable amnesiac colleague." Planned length: closer to 10 minutes, 15 at most.

## Audience

Deliberately mixed and reusable. Primary: first-time founders, students and postdocs at an energy-sector startup accelerator (next week). Also: SME coaching and consulting prospects at the Academy workshop the week after: tech, non-tech and tech-adjacent workers, including people with zero coding experience who only use ChatGPT. The analogy must land for all of them.

## Learning goal

The viewer realizes that capturing AI's value means reinvesting effort as new skills and disciplines, for themselves and across their organization, because AI relocates the work rather than removing it.

## Actionables

1. Next time you reach for AI on something real, before you type a word, jot down what you'd tell a brand-new colleague on day one: the context, the tools they'd need, the brief, and how you'll check their work.

## Hook

Open on the gap between the hype ("become 20x more productive with AI") and the reality that your own prompt attempts just don't deliver. Leave the "why" hanging in the room, then switch into the thought experiment.

## Red thread

AI underdelivers because you prompt it like a tool, when the task is to onboard it like a colleague, and onboarding requires management skills, not execution skills.

## Analogy

Onboarding a new, highly capable colleague on their first day.

Thought experiment: you must onboard a new colleague to plan a company-wide team-building event (Sommerfest or Christmas party) for about 100 people. It's their first day, it's urgent, and a fully structured plan is due by end of day for your team lead or the CEO. Their profile: knows essentially the entire written world, extremely knowledgeable, extremely confident, eager to please. Ask the room to take a second: how would you onboard them? Run it as a lived use case; do not dissect it into labelled buckets during the experiment.

Resolution: no 1:1 mapping. Land it light: you didn't do the task, you managed it; that shift from doing to managing is what working with AI demands. Then a teaser naming the disciplines, one line each: prompt engineering, phrasing the ask as a clear and unambiguous brief; context engineering, the background you hand over (budget, headcount, culture, past events); harness engineering, the tools and environment you give access to (booking system, catering contacts, calendar); and graph engineering, the newest term, managing the workflow across agents, project management for AI, emerging since mid-2026 and still loosely defined.

Closing bridge: it feels like a lot of work, "but here's the twist: AI can help you onboard AI." Points lightly toward grill-me, worded as a standalone ending, not a cliffhanger.

## Research questions

1. Why do others frame working with AI as onboarding a colleague? Collect sources proposing a similar model (Matt Pocock confirmed; a consultant said the same in a company workshop), to show it's an established lens.
2. Why do individuals fail to reach the promised AI productivity boost?
3. Why do organizations fail to reach the promised AI productivity boost?
4. Why does richer context and a clearer brief measurably improve AI output?
5. Why does unchecked AI work carry real risk?
6. Why is effective AI delegation a staff-level management discipline, and how does that reconcile with findings that AI boosts novices more on simple tasks? The claim is not that seniors have these skills; the task demands management discipline, which merely correlates with experience.
