## Research

- [Why do others frame working with AI as onboarding a colleague?](research/AIE-0001__ai-as-onboarding-a-colleague/research.md) — Vendors and practitioners call AI a new colleague because it has broad ability but no knowledge of your work, so you must supply context, a brief and checks.
- [Why do individuals fail to reach the promised AI productivity boost?](research/AIE-0002__individual-ai-productivity-gap/research.md) — Individuals miss the promised AI boost because gains depend on task fit, context and checking: measured effects are mixed and smaller than the hype, and self-reports overstate them.
- [Why do organizations fail to reach the promised AI productivity boost?](research/AIE-0003__organizational-ai-productivity-gap/research.md) — AI value needs complementary investment in workflows and skills that adoption alone skips, so measured firm gains are small; the "95% of pilots fail" figure is disputed, but the gap is real.
- [Why does richer context and a clearer brief measurably improve AI output?](research/AIE-0004__context-and-brief-improve-ai-output/research.md) — Relevant, specific context and a fully specified brief measurably lift LLM output, but padding, noise and boilerplate overviews do not help and can hurt.
- [Why does unchecked AI work carry real risk?](research/AIE-0005__risks-of-unchecked-ai-output/research.md) — Unchecked AI output has caused documented harm, from sanctioned fake citations to less secure code written with more confidence, so someone must verify it.
- [Why is effective AI delegation a management discipline rather than a skill of seniority, given that AI boosts novices more on simple tasks?](research/AIE-0006__ai-delegation-as-management-skill/research.md) — Delegating real work to AI takes management disciplines (briefing, context, verification) that experience merely correlates with, and novice-boost studies measure bounded tasks, not that discipline.
