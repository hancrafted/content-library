# Context window cornerstone

Read before placing, changing or testing the context window Explorable explanation (`src/explorable-explanations/context/`) or the call transcript (`src/episodes/components/slide-shared/call-transcript/`). Binding rules: [FE-007 Module Layering](../../../.archgate/adrs/FE-007-module-layering.md) §5, [FE-006 Server/Client Boundary](../../../.archgate/adrs/FE-006-server-client-boundary.md) §5 and [FE-011 Design Tokens](../../../.archgate/adrs/FE-011-design-tokens.md). This guide holds the detail that does not bind.

## Placing it

- A Slide renders `<Context preset="slide-4" locale={locale} />` from `context.tsx`, the server entry. It reads the Preset, the chat and the words at build time and hands them, serializable, to the one client leaf, `context.client.tsx`.
- `words` overrides any word of the part's own: `<Context preset="slide-6" locale={locale} words={{ contextWindow: t('window') }} />`.
- Static bars: `<ContextBarsPreset preset="slide-11-after" locale={locale} />` draws every bar of a `minis` Preset horizontally with the pure view `context-bar-view.tsx`, server-only, no client JavaScript. A bar is a chat at a `step`, optionally keeping only some of it in colour (`lit`: Simple categories, `litFrom`: a step id, `ring`: step ids; the rest fades). `contextBarFigures({ preset, locale })` returns the window, buffer and token figures of a Preset's first bar, so a Slide never writes "128K of 200K" or "16.5K" by hand.
- The session bar: `<SessionBarPreset locale={locale} at="what-fills-the-window" />` (`at={null}` is a fresh session) draws the audience's attention as one segment per Section, sized by its share, filled up to and including `at`, the current one highlighted and named. `at="end"` (`SESSION_END`) is the full bar of the closing Slide. The plan (each Section's `id` and `share`) is the config's `session:` key; `words.session.sections` holds what each Section's opener hangs under its current segment, so a new Section adds one entry to each file; each segment takes a tint from calm to hot by its place. The hero and every Section opener of ai-attention-span place it. Pure model: `sessionParts` in `context-bars.pure.ts`; view: `session-bar-view.tsx`.
- A `*-view.tsx` file is a pure view: no hooks, no handlers, no `'use client'`, no hook or `.client` import. ESLint refuses them (`tests/boundaries/pure-view-lint.test.ts` probes it), and a renderToStaticMarkup test holds the markup. The interactive context window reuses the view's `BarBuffer` and `BarLines` inside its own bar.
- The call transcript (Slides 1, 2 and 6): `<CallTranscript label={t('call.label')} size="md" lines={[{ who: 'you', text: t('bubbles.0'), light: 'lit' }, …]} />`. One line is `{ who: 'you' | 'sister' | 'ai' | 'tool'; text; light: 'lit' | 'faded' | 'standout' }`; `size` is `md` (the Scene) or `sm` (beside another picture). `CallBubble` and `callBubbleClass` place a single bubble. Every bubble carries `data-who`, `data-light` and `data-index`, so a Slide's own step leaf and CSS can fade or light bubbles; the transcript owns no words.
- The Context window page, `/explorable-explanations/context-window/` (the Resource `src/episodes/resources/context-window/`, see the GLOSSARY), shows the cornerstone whole on its "Every part and control" Slide, Preset `gallery-full`: every part, every control, the summer party at step 25 of 28, just past its compaction. Preview a change to the visual there before it reaches an Episode; send one Slide by its anchor, `/explorable-explanations/context-window/#context-window--every-part-and-control`. A new way to show the cornerstone is one Slide folder in its Section.

## Data

- `context-preset.config.yml` holds the numbers: the starting instructions, the window sizes (200K with a 16.5K buffer, 1M with 33K), the smart-zone lines, the chats, the step notes (`mayFill`, `refs`, `big`), the Brief's attention split for step 21 (start 20, middle 50, end 30 of 100 units, as the `attention.summer-party.21` override), the defaults and one Preset per Slide state. It also holds the English `words` and `story`; `context-preset.config.de.yml` holds only the German `words` and `story`, under the same keys (a test holds the key sets equal). The locale picks the pair at build time.
- A Preset's fields: `chat`, `step`, `depth` (simple, technical), `pinned`, `show` (chat, timeline, context, category, panel, attention), `fade` (the chat dims each message by its attention; on whenever the Attention part is shown), `attention.layout` (`list`, `pinned` or `compressed`, default `list`), `controls` (see Controls below), `order` (the parts in chip and grid order, every part once; default `chat, timeline, context, category, panel, attention`), `timelineLocked`, `windows` (the window sizes, the first shown at rest), `motion` and `height` (the main row's height in rem on a wide container, 38 by default; slide-3 sets 26.6, 30% less, so the cards below show on a laptop). `presetProblems` refuses a step outside the timeline, an unknown part, control or Attention layout, an `order` that does not list every part once, a field a control does not take, anything but `show` on `'*'`, a pinned part not in the chat, a window switch with fewer than two window sizes, a height that is not a positive number, more than two lines or a line above its window, and a fill that enters the buffer anywhere but right before a compaction.

### Controls

`context-controls.pure.ts` is the registry: one typed definition per control (`id`, `type`, `placement`, label and tooltip words, a lucide icon name, the default `show`), by type `toggle` (flips a shown-state key), `segmented`, `action` (download, upload) or `chips` (the parts group). The Preset's `controls` is a map from control id to overrides, merged key by key over `defaults.controls`; `'*'` may carry only `show` and applies to every control the Preset does not name itself. Presets state only what differs (slide-3: `{ '*': { show: false } }`; slide-5 adds `window: { show: true }`; slide-9 adds `zone: { on: false }`). `presetProblems` validates the map against each control's type.

| id                      | where                        | what it does                                                                                                                                                           | default                       |
| ----------------------- | ---------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| `parts`                 | toolbar                      | the Show chips (Conversation, Timeline, Context window, Context category, Details, Attention); `reorder: false` turns the drag and drop off (see Reordering the parts) | shown                         |
| `technical`             | toolbar                      | Technical for every part                                                                                                                                               | shown                         |
| `download`, `upload`    | toolbar                      | save or open a Claude Code log                                                                                                                                         | download shown, upload hidden |
| `zone`                  | head, side column            | shows or hides the smart-zone labels and the limit label; the dashed lines stay and the bar widens; `on: false` starts it hidden                                       | shown                         |
| `conversationTechnical` | conversation head, `</>`     | Technical for the conversation only; the toolbar's Technical keeps the last value it set                                                                               | shown                         |
| `windowTechnical`       | head, `</>`                  | Technical for the context window only; the toolbar's Technical keeps the last value it set                                                                             | shown                         |
| `categoryTechnical`     | context category head, `</>` | Technical for the context category only; the toolbar's Technical keeps the last value it set                                                                           | shown                         |
| `meter`                 | head, readout                | the percent and "used of window" figures; `show: false` hides them (Slide 9, where the point is that you cannot watch the meter); `'*'` leaves it alone                | shown                         |
| `window`                | head                         | the `200K \| 1M` segmented switch (plain `128K of 200K` without it); needs two window sizes                                                                            | hidden                        |

The head reads `Context window (64%)   128K of [200K | 1M]` and then the icon buttons, each with a tooltip, an `aria-label` and `aria-pressed`. The old Explain/Attention switch is gone (Attention is its own part); the meter flag is gone (`zone`).

- The chat is `summer-party.jsonl` and `summer-party.de.jsonl`, read by `fromClaudeLog` and written by `toClaudeLog`; `fromClaudeLog(toClaudeLog(chat))` deep-equals the chat. The two logs hold the same sizes; only the text differs.

### The log subset

The cornerstone reads the lines of a Claude Code session log (`~/.claude/projects/<project>/<session>.jsonl`) that shape the context window, and skips every other line, a line that is not JSON and a side-chain line.

| Line                                             | Fields read                                                                      | Becomes                                                                                                   |
| ------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `type: user`, string or `text` content           | `uuid`, `message.content`, `isCompactSummary`, `isMeta`                          | a `you` step; a compaction summary; a hidden or meta text becomes a System reminder of the step before it |
| `type: user`, `tool_result` content              | `tool_use_id`, `content`                                                         | the result of the tool step that called it                                                                |
| `type: assistant`                                | `requestId`, `message.content` (`text`, `tool_use`, `thinking`), `message.usage` | an `ai` step, or one `tool` step per request's tool calls                                                 |
| `type: attachment`                               | `attachment.type`, `attachment.content`                                          | `nested_memory` is a memory step, any other a System reminder                                             |
| `type: system`, `subtype: compact_boundary`      | `compactMetadata.trigger`, `preTokens`, `postTokens`                             | a `compact` step                                                                                          |
| `type: system`, `subtype: microcompact_boundary` | `microcompactMetadata.tokensSaved`                                               | a `cleared` step: the oldest tool results leave the window                                                |

Sizes: each request's context (`input_tokens + cache_read_input_tokens + cache_creation_input_tokens`) is exact; its growth since the last request is shared over the blocks that arrived, by their length, and a reply's `output_tokens` over its parts. A summary is the context after the compaction less the starting instructions and memory. A System reminder is estimated at four characters a token. A log opened in the browser is sized against the chat's own starting instructions.

## Parts and state

- `context.client.tsx` is the one client leaf and the parent. It owns a reducer (`context-state.pure.ts`) and hands each part only its slice and callbacks, never the whole state: `conversation.tsx`, `timeline.tsx`, `context-window.tsx` (Technical in `context-window-grid.tsx`, the zoom in `context-window-loupe.tsx`), `context-category.tsx` (the context category: usage by category, with a bold title), `details.tsx` (Explain: what the lit part is), `attention.tsx` (the 100 units over the steps in the window; its own show state and `renderMode`). `context-slices.tsx` builds each slice; the prop types are in `context-parts.types.ts`.
- A step is one message of the chat the context window plays: the starting instructions, a memory file, a turn, a tool call, a compaction or a clearing; a Preset opens at one (`step: 21`). The step on screen is the visible step: the preview while the reader scrubs, else the committed step. The timeline's read-out and the status line follow the committed step only.
- The step view (`stepView` in `context-step.pure.ts`) is everything more than one part reads at the step on screen: the visible and committed step, the frame, the window size, the readout, the attention (the Preset's own split for its chat at that step, else the curve), the lit and ghost keys, and the steps gone from the window. The client leaf builds it once per render as `m.view`; parts never derive these themselves. A derivation only one part uses stays in that part (`usageRows`, `gridBoxes`), and view transitions stay in the reducer. The step view hands back the same frames and attention while only the lighting changes, so a hover redraws only the items whose `on` flipped and the compressed Attention list does not refit.
- Props down, events up: a part reports a lit key with `onLight(key | null)` and a click with `onPin(key, target)`; `use-lighting.ts` is the wiring each part puts on its own root, and `use-window-events.ts` the parent's side (the callbacks, a click outside that unpins, Escape). `context-category` is a part of its own with its own callbacks. Its grid slot is beside `context-window` in Simple and beneath it, in the window's column, in Technical (`columns` writes two rows); in Technical its Messages group is a `<details>` closed at first, in Simple there is none. Every part with a Simple and a Technical view (conversation, context window, category) has its own `</>` in its head; the timeline has no Technical view of its own, and Attention and Details have none.
- Hover is brushing and linking. The parent holds the hovered and pinned key; a part turns it into one `on` boolean per item and passes it to a memoised item, so a hover redraws only the items whose `on` flipped. There is no store. `context.hover-budget.test.ts` guards this by rendering each part for two lit keys and counting the items whose markup differs.
- renderMode (`simple` or `technical`) is one global value plus per-part overrides in the parent's state (`depth` and `renderModes`). The toolbar's Technical sets every part and drops the overrides; `SET_PART_MODE` sets one part. The latest action wins. A part's own look keys on its own `data-depth` or `data-view`, not on the root's.

## Reordering the parts

- The reader drags the part chips in the toolbar's Show group with the mouse (`window-chip-drag.ts`, native pointer events, no dependency). A drag starts after 4 px with the primary button; every chip the pointer passes the middle of takes the dragged chip's place, live. The click that ends a drag does not toggle the chip. `controls.parts.reorder: false` turns it off.
- The order is `ViewState.order`, started from the Preset's `order`, moved by the `REORDER` event (`moveTo` in `context-state.pure.ts`) and never stored in the browser: a reload returns to the Preset.
- `Parts` renders the grid children keyed by part id in state order, and `columns` writes the grid areas in the same order, so React moves the DOM nodes without remounting and Tab order follows the picture. The category has its own slot, so dragging its chip moves it on screen like any other part's.
- Moving a node resets the chat's scroll and restarts CSS animations under it. `window-reorder.ts` saves the chat's `scrollTop` before the event and, in a layout effect after the new order, restores it and finishes every finite animation (the entrance and the bar and grid morph), so nothing replays.
- Known accessibility gap: there is no keyboard or button alternative, by Han's decision, so WCAG 2.5.7 Dragging Movements (AA) is not met. It is an enhancement only: every part stays reachable and toggleable without it.
- Browser tests (`context.browser.test.ts`, "reordering the parts"): a real mouse drag moves the chip, the same DOM nodes survive, the chat keeps its scroll and no animation runs afterwards.

## Attention layouts

- `attention.layout` is a Preset field separate from renderMode, so the toolbar's Technical never undoes it. `list` scrolls when the rows overflow. `pinned` makes the first and last three rows `position: sticky` (CSS only). `compressed` gives each row its natural height while they fit; once they do not, the list fills the height the card leaves it, to the last row: the first and last three rows keep their natural height and the rows between compress in proportion to their share, 4 px at least. A row too thin to show its name whole (`data-tight`) hides the name, so no row shows a cut-through sliver of text (slide-4 uses it).
- The shares come from a distribution curve (`attention-curve.pure.ts`) over the rows of the window, oldest first: `start` (the first 3 rows, default 20, split 70/20/10 oldest first by the Preset's `attention.falloff.start`, so only the very first row (the starting instructions, the attention sink) stays high while the memory and the user's first message fall towards the middle level, each weight jittered up to 15% by a random seeded from the ids), `newest` (the last 3, 35, split 50/30/20 newest first by `attention.falloff.newest`), `recent` (the 7 before them, so the 4th to 10th newest, 20) and `middle` (the rest, 25); the defaults follow the qualitative serial-position shape (human free-recall recency and primacy, LLM "Lost in the Middle" U-shape [Liu et al. 2023], and initial attention sinks [Xiao et al. 2023]), where a deterministic random seeded from the step ids gives one or two rows a clearly larger share. An empty bucket's share goes to the others in proportion; on a small window start beats newest beats recent. It works for any chat, an opened Claude Code log included. A Preset sets it in `attention.curve: { start, recent, middle, newest }` (whole numbers summing to 100, validated; `attention.layout` alone keeps the default curve and falloff; each `falloff` list is three positive numbers; a Preset may name only one of `start` and `newest`); the per-step splits under `attention:` in the config stay as overrides for the steps they name. Hovering or focusing a row shows its percentage at the right of its bar (`[data-pct]`, out of flow, so nothing shifts; in every layout, thin compressed rows included). Every row carries `data-attention-bucket` (`start`, `recent`, `middle` or `newest`), a stable hook for a Slide that marks the middle.
- The `attentionMenu` control (a "⋯" button right of the Attention title, shown unless the Preset hides it) opens a popover in the Episode portal root with two sections (all of it view state, transient, FE-001: a reload returns to the Preset's). The distribution section has four labelled range sliders that always sum to 100: moving one spreads the difference evenly over the others, clamped at 0 (`setShare`). A pen at its top right turns the percentages into number inputs with no validation while typing, and the pen into a check whose tooltip reads "Your input will be normalized to 100."; saving runs `normalizeCurve` (bad or negative entries count as 0, the rest scale to 100; all zero gives the default curve). While an explicit per-step split is in force the distribution section is left out. The layout section toggles `list`, `pinned` and `compressed` (`ViewState.layout`, started from `attention.layout`); Slide 5 starts `compressed`, so the starting rows and the newest are always in view.
- The bars scale adaptively (`axisMaxOf`): the track has a fixed width and its right end is the axis, labelled with the current maximum (`data-axis-max`). The maximum steps 100, 50, 40, 30, 20 percent (floor 20) to the smallest that holds the largest share, so bars stay long as attention spreads. The bar's `transform` transition (`--att-scale`) animates a scale change; reduced motion sets it to 0.
- Compressed magnifies like the Mac dock: rows within three either side of the pointer grow towards a full text row and the others shrink so the total height stays fixed. The maths is `attention-dock.pure.ts`; `attention-dock.ts` writes the heights to the DOM from one `requestAnimationFrame` per pointer move, so React never redraws the rows for it. Under reduced motion the stylesheet drops the height transition and the rows snap.
- Hovering or clicking a row lights its chat message (the parent's lit key) and scrolls it into view (`onReveal`, then `onPin` for a click).

## Manual testing criteria

Run on the dev page, in Chrome, light and dark, with the tab in front (a background tab never fires `requestAnimationFrame`).

- Phone width (390px): the parts stack; the timeline and the bar run across; dragging the timeline commits, tapping a part pins it, nothing previews on touch.
- VoiceOver: the toolbar reads its controls and pressed states; the timeline reads the step sentence (`aria-valuetext`); each step commit is announced once by the status line; a bubble reads its category and text.
- Keyboard only (no mouse): Tab reaches the toolbar, the chat bubbles, the timeline, the bar or grid (one stop) and the category rows. Arrows step the timeline and move between blocks in the bar or grid; focus lights like hover. Enter pins, Escape unpins. The focus ring (ring token) shows beside the lit outline (primary).
- Reduced motion: bubbles, blocks and boxes change at once, no entrance and no morph.
- Dark mode: the terminal stays dark, every category tint holds against the card, the smart-zone label reads on primary when crossed.
- Print: the toolbar, the hint, the ghost and the tip are gone; the rest prints at its step.
- The browser lane: `npm run build`, then `npm run test:browser` (Playwright, against the built export; the Vitest browser lane is gone). `context.browser.test.ts` pins only the behaviours the user corrected after looking at them (the zoom staying open at a slow pace, the lens stroke, the Attention dock, the hover percentage, the tooltip, the compressed list), each titled in the user's words, on `gallery-full` of the Context window page and on Slide 5 of the AI attention span Episode. The logic under them is in the `*.pure.test.ts` files. Waits are retrying assertions or `data-motion="settled"` (ARCH-003 §5): every Context element that animates on a user action or an entrance (the parts, bubbles, blocks, rows, timeline thumb, lens, loupe, toolbar buttons) reports `data-motion` (`running|settled`) through `useMotion` (`src/shared/hooks/use-motion.ts`, via `moves.client.tsx`): running from the state change, counting its own transitions and keyframe animations, settling on its own when none starts; the endless session pulse and the `*-view.tsx` call bar, which render on the server, carry none, while the Preset's motion style (`crisp|rise|spring`) is `data-motion-style` on the root. axe-core over both Explorable explanation pages runs in `tests/browser/a11y.browser.test.ts`; the hover budget is held by `context.hover-budget.test.ts` in the fast suite.

## Walkthroughs

Each starts from its Preset at rest on the dev page.

- **slide-3** (chat, timeline and context, step 9, the budget pinned, no toolbar, 26.6rem high). Hover step 4 on the timeline: the chat shows four messages, the hint goes, nothing commits. Leave: back to step 9. Press Escape: the budget unpins.
- **slide-4** (Attention, category hidden, runs to the compaction at step 24 of `summer-compact`; scrub back to step 21 for the faded middle). After the compaction Attention is `m1` 15, the summary 45, the newest message 40. At step 21 the budget message (m3) is the faded one with the dashed "the AI may fill this gap" note. Hover any block: Attention keeps its card, the lit step's row turns bold. Technical: unit values appear beside the bars.
- **slide-5** (controls hidden, `window` control on). Switch to 1M: the same chat fills 13%, the 150K line is far above the fill and no line is crossed. Back to 200K: 64%, past Matt Pocock's 80K line.
- **slide-6** (Technical grid only, locked). Hover a box: it and its row light; lighting is never locked. No toolbar and no timeline; the window's own `</>` stays and flips it to Simple.
- **slide-7** (Technical, starting instructions pinned). Hover one web-pages box: only the tool-result boxes of step 8 light, and the Tool results row. Click the Skills row, then Tool results, then Messages: each lights only its boxes. Simple: the rows fold into plain categories and the grid becomes the bar; the pin stays. Collapse the chat: a rail of ticks. Click the head's side-column button: the smart-zone labels and the limit label go, the lines stay, the bar widens. `</>` in a part's head makes only that part Technical.
- **slide-7-parts** (Simple, starting instructions pinned; the context window, the context category and the details, no chat and no timeline). Each of the window and the category has its own `</>`; the details have none.
- **slide-8** (Technical, step 12, the catering PDF read pinned, no chat). The panel shows tool calls 200 and tool results 55K. Step back to 10: the PDF read is pinned but not there yet, a dashed outline and "Not in the context yet at this step". Step to 11: the jump lands, past Pocock's line.
- **slide-9-before, slide-9-chat, slide-9-after** (locked, smart-zone column off). Before: step 23, the fill inside the hatched buffer, the contract read (m26) pinned. Chat: step 24, the compacted chat, earlier bubbles dashed as gone. After: step 24, the summary pinned, the free space back.
- **Scrub back while pinned** (slide-8): pin m11, step to 5 (dashed outline), hover step 16 (preview shows it landed), commit 11 (it lands in full colour), click elsewhere (unpins).
- **Download** (every Preset whose toolbar shows): the button saves the chat as a Claude Code log; opening that file with an upload-enabled Preset (`upload: true`) replays the same chat.
